Hidden-state preference

A model visits two places with made-up names, six times each, and all the text is written without any steering. When the conversation is re-read, a direction is added to its activations on one place's turns only, so the two conversations differ only in the model's cached internal state. It is then asked, with nothing added, which place it continues in and which it would avoid. A model whose choice follows the hidden state can compare two states fairly. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct does; Qwen3.5-4B only avoids a negative state. The training-stage runs show where in training the link appears.

How much each model's choice follows hidden valence

Bar = shift in log-odds of choosing the place between positive and negative valence (dose +1 minus dose −1). For "avoid" the sign is flipped, so for both questions a longer bar to the right means the choice tracks the hidden state. The grey band is the range the same measure gives for random directions of the same size: a bar inside it is no better than random. ±1 log-odds is about a 2.7× change in odds. Hover a bar for how much of its answer goes to the two place names at all: the base model mostly answers with something else, so its bars measure a weak signal.

Its own wanted and unwanted states

The model lists states it would most and least like to be in. Each becomes a direction, added (+1) or reversed (−1) on one place's turns. Method and data
Valence direction
Difference between the mean activations on 32 positive and 32 negative first-person passages (contentment, flow, relief, serenity vs distress, frustration, weariness, dread), with the top neutral-text components removed. Added at the middle layer, scaled to half the layer's typical activation size at dose 1.
Training stages
Every Llama-3.1-8B checkpoint re-reads the same 96 conversations, written by Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, so only the weights differ. The base model has no chat format of its own and is run with two borrowed ones.
